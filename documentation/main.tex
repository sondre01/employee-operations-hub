\section{How the Platform Works}

\subsection{The Three Main Parts in Plain English}
Think of the Employee Operations Hub like a friendly modern workplace app. It consists of three simple building blocks working together behind the scenes:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Web App (Frontend)}: The website you open in your browser. It gives you a clean, easy-to-use screen where you can clock in, see announcements, or submit support tickets.
    \item \textbf{The Central Server (Backend API)}: The ``brain'' behind the scenes. When you click a button (like ``Clock In'' or ``Submit Ticket''), the server checks who you are, verifies your permissions, and carries out your request safely.
    \item \textbf{The Storage (Database)}: The secure digital filing cabinet where employee profiles, attendance records, support tickets, and laptop assignments are kept.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Instant Start vs. Production Database}
To make testing as painless as possible, the application offers two easy database modes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Instant Local Mode (SQLite)}: When running on your laptop, the app automatically creates a local database file (\texttt{employee\_hub.db}) pre-filled with demo employees, sample tickets, and company policies. You do not need to install any database software.
    \item \textbf{Production Mode (PostgreSQL)}: For hosting on a shared company server, the app connects to a robust PostgreSQL database with automatic data safety and backups.
\end{itemize}

\section{User Experience \& Access Levels}

\subsection{Two Main Areas of the Platform}
The top navigation bar lets users easily switch between two clear areas, depending on their role:

\subsubsection{Area 1: The Employee Portal (For Everyone)}
Every team member—whether an internal staff member or a developer dedicated to a client—uses this portal for their day-to-day work:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Main Dashboard}:
    An intuitive, all-in-one view that shows you everything at a glance without having to scroll:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Personal Info Card}: Shows your job title, team, department, manager, and any company hardware currently in your care.
        \item \textbf{Time Clock \& Dual World Clocks}: Displays live clocks for both \textbf{Manila (PH)} and \textbf{Copenhagen (DK)}, plus one-click buttons to Clock In, take a Break, start Lunch, return from Lunch, and Clock Out.
        \item \textbf{Company Announcements}: Important updates, memos, and news posted by People Operations.
        \item \textbf{Onboarding Checklist}: A visual progress ring showing how much of your required paperwork and onboarding tasks are complete.
        \item \textbf{My Pending Requests}: A quick summary of your submitted shift adjustments or overtime requests awaiting manager approval.
        \item \textbf{Upcoming Payroll Schedule}: Clear countdown dates showing when timesheets close, when approvals must be finished, and when pay is credited.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Attendance Tab}: A clear calendar ledger of all your past clock-ins, break times, and total monthly hours.
    \item \textbf{Time Requests Tab}: A simple form to request overtime pre-approval or correct an accidental missed punch.
    \item \textbf{Holidays \& Policies}: See upcoming holidays in both countries and review company policies.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Area 2: The Service Desk (For Support \& Operations Teams)}
Visible only to authorized internal team members (such as IT, HR, and Operations). It provides five straightforward screens:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Queue}: View, triage, and solve incoming support requests from staff.
    \item \textbf{Analytics}: Clear, visual summaries of how fast tickets are resolved and which issues are most common.
    \item \textbf{Departments}: View and organize support teams (such as IT, HR, Finance, and DevOps).
    \item \textbf{Access}: Safely assign or update helpdesk permissions for staff members.
    \item \textbf{Roster}: View the list of team members and their client assignments.
\end{itemize}

\section{What Information the System Keeps Track Of}

The system securely organizes all essential workplace data into six clear categories:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Employee Records}:
    Stores your name, work email, job title, department, assigned client (if applicable), and encrypted login credentials.
    
    \item \textbf{Departments}:
    The company teams that handle requests, such as \textit{IT Support}, \textit{Human Resources}, \textit{Finance}, and \textit{DevOps}.
    
    \item \textbf{Helpdesk Tickets}:
    Each request includes a title, detailed description, urgency (Low, Medium, High, or Critical), category (IT or HR), the current status (\textit{Open}, \textit{In Progress}, or \textit{Resolved}), and resolution notes.
    
    \item \textbf{Company Equipment (Laptops \& Hardware)}:
    Keeps track of company-owned laptops and devices, including make and model (e.g., MacBook Pro), serial number, asset tag, date assigned, and return condition.
    
    \item \textbf{Cross-Border Holidays}:
    Maintains the calendar of official public holidays in both the Philippines and Denmark, noting whether a holiday is a working or non-working day.
    
    \item \textbf{Company Policies \& Compliance}:
    Houses official company handbooks and guidelines. Tracks when each employee reads and digitally acknowledges required company policies.
\end{itemize}

\section{How to Run and Test the Application}

You do not need to install complicated database servers or spend hours configuring settings. The application is designed to start in seconds.

\subsection{Option 1: The Quickest Way (Run Locally)}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Start the Backend Server}:
    Open your terminal in the project root folder and run:
    \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
    npm run start:backend
    \end{lstlisting}
    \begin{tipbox}[Zero Database Setup Required]
    The server will start at \textbf{\url{http://localhost:5000}}. It automatically creates a fresh local database with all test accounts, sample tickets, and policies ready to use. You can also view the interactive API screen at \textbf{\url{http://localhost:5000/swagger}}.
    \end{tipbox}

    \item \textbf{Start the Web Portal (Frontend)}:
    Open a second terminal window and run:
    \begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
    npm --prefix frontend run dev
    \end{lstlisting}
    Open your browser and visit:
    \begin{center}
    \textbf{\url{http://localhost:5173}}
    \end{center}
    You can now log in using any of the demo accounts listed in Section 3 (Password: \texttt{Password123!}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Option 2: Run Everything with Docker in One Command}

If you have Docker installed and want to run both the frontend, backend, and a production PostgreSQL database together:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
docker compose up --build
\end{lstlisting}

Once finished, open your browser:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Web Portal}: \url{http://localhost:8080}
    \item \textbf{Backend Server}: \url{http://localhost:5000}
\end{itemize}

\section{Why This Helps Cross-Border Teams}

Managing a team where developers in the Philippines collaborate daily with clients and managers in Denmark comes with unique everyday challenges. Here is how the Hub solves them:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{No More Time Zone Confusion}:
    The dashboard shows live digital clocks for both Manila and Copenhagen side-by-side. Everyone knows instantly whether their colleagues are online, starting their workday, or out on lunch.

    \item \textbf{Side-by-Side Holiday Calendars}:
    Danish and Philippine public holidays often fall on completely different dates. The platform displays both national schedules in one place, clearly highlighting Danish holidays (like \textit{Store Bededag} or \textit{Ascension Day}) and Philippine observances (like \textit{Araw ng Kagitingan}), so project deliverables are never caught off guard.

    \item \textbf{Clear Hardware Tracking}:
    When high-value laptops (such as Apple MacBook Pros) are issued to offshore developers, the platform records the asset tags, serial numbers, issue dates, and return condition, keeping equipment inventory accountable and transparent.

    \item \textbf{Philippine Legal \& HR Support}:
    Philippine team members can quickly file tickets for Certificate of Employment requests, annual tax forms (BIR 2316), and government contributions (SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG).

    \item \textbf{Client Privacy \& Peace of Mind}:
    Developers assigned to a specific client see only their personal portal and their assigned work. They cannot see tickets or information belonging to other client accounts, keeping client projects strictly private.
\end{itemize}
